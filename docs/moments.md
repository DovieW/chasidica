## A worked example: the courtyard evening

This is a candidate experience to explore, not a scripted event promised for the game.

**Setup:** the player has adapted an unused courtyard and supported a small bakery. A teacher wants to welcome visiting families. Several residents already know one another through earlier projects.

**Player agency:** choose whether to host the gathering, keep it modest or make more room, and whom to ask for help. A small event should still be worthwhile.

**Independent contributions:** the bakery owner offers refreshments. Someone brings extra benches. A guest suggests a tune. These contributions depend on participating people and their relationships; every campaign need not receive the same sequence.

**Visible payoff:** the courtyard fills, nearby rooms light up, conversations form, and people sing. The player can linger and observe. There is no compulsory emergency attached to the successful evening.

**Persistent effects:** a friendship, recurring activity, or local tradition may begin. The courtyard becomes a familiar location. A visitor may later invite someone back.

**Long callback:** much later, the same tune is heard at a distant branch. The game can show its connection to that earlier gathering because it retained the relevant history.

**Alternate outcomes:** a quieter evening might deepen a few relationships; a larger one might create new contacts; another player might use the courtyard for a different project entirely. Each approach needs a satisfying identity.

## How to develop an idea

For each promising moment, ask:

- What existing situation makes it possible?
- What can the player discover, choose, encourage, or simply witness?
- What will actually be visible or audible?
- What makes this instance specific to the campaign?
- Does it create a new possibility, a relationship, a tradition, or a remembered detail?
- How can it vary on another playthrough?
- Can it be missed or declined without making the player feel punished?
- Which cultural details need checking before production?

A useful next exercise is to choose a few contrasting moments and work backward to the smallest set of people, places, and rules that could support them.
