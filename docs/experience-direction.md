# Graphics, music, and the whole experience

Planning notes: 2026-09-06.

Historical discussion guide. The options below are superseded by [the selected presentation specification](presentation.md) and [design v1](../DESIGN.md); retain them as rationale and unused ideas, not an additional feature list.

## Priority

Graphics and music need careful attention as part of what makes Chasidica enjoyable. The player's actions, presentation, and feature set should support a coherent experience.

The importance of graphics and music has been explicitly raised. The directions and options below are proposals to discuss, not approved production choices.

## Start with a scene

A useful shared reference is an evening gathering in the community's first courtyard.

The player has helped create the place and chosen how to use it. Familiar people arrive. Some bring things or help independently. Lights come on, conversations gather, and a melody begins. The player can recognize what their decisions contributed and enjoy watching the result.

This gives us one scene against which to judge the game together:

- Gameplay: was preparing and shaping the gathering interesting?
- Graphics: is the place appealing, readable, and full of life?
- Animation: do people convey personality and react to one another?
- Sound: does the space feel occupied, and can the player hear where activity is happening?
- Music: does the occasion have a recognizable emotional identity?
- Continuity: does anything meaningful remain after it ends?

We can later compare this with an ordinary street and a much larger celebration. These are proposed references for discussion, not work already started.

## Visual decisions

The neighborhood view is the starting direction. Its final art style still needs to be chosen; the existing sketch explores layout and interaction.

| Decision | Options worth exploring | Initial proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall style | Warm detailed miniatures; expressive cartoon forms; a more naturalistic treatment. | Warm miniature buildings and expressive people, with enough detail to reward looking closely. |
| Camera | Primarily a fixed tilted view; freely rotating view; a few selected close views. | Keep the main neighborhood view easy to read and allow closer inspection of an occasion or place. |
| Character emphasis | Mostly crowds; a small recognizable cast among crowds; detailed individual control. | A recognizable cast within a lively population. |
| Environmental detail | Broad shapes and color; decorative detail; substantial interactive interiors. | Distinctive buildings, signs, windows, courtyards, furnishings, and a few meaningful interior spaces. |
| Player expression | Choose functional upgrades; decorate and name places; detailed construction. | Let a few visible choices express the community's identity before deciding the scope of a building editor. |

Light, movement, composition, and small behaviors should help make ordinary observation pleasant. School dismissal, deliveries, a person stopping to greet someone, and people setting up a room are candidates for the first visual studies.

We still need to decide how calendar time, seasons, day and night, and the pace of a visible scene relate to one another.

## Music and sound decisions

Treat the soundtrack and the sounds of communal life as important parts of the game's identity.

Three roles are worth considering:

- Everyday ambience: footsteps, doors, street activity, nearby conversations, and sound from occupied places.
- Background score: music that supports observation, planning, anticipation, and the passage of time.
- Music within the world: people singing or playing at an occasion the player can find and watch.

| Decision | What we need to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Musical identity | The desired relationship between niggunim, communal singing, modern Hasidic music, and an original game score. |
| Everyday intensity | How frequently music plays, how prominent it is, and how much space belongs to ambience and quiet. |
| Occasions | Which situations deserve distinct musical treatment, and what feels appropriate to the community represented. |
| Adaptation | Whether arrangements change with the size, atmosphere, participants, or history of an occasion. |
| Lasting identity | Whether a court can acquire recognizable melodies or musical traditions that return elsewhere. |
| Player control | Separate control of score, singing, ambience, and effects, with support for noticing events without depending on sound alone. |

A promising musical idea is to let a modest melody become associated with a remembered event, then return in different settings as the community grows. This remains a creative proposal; repertoire, composition, performance, and production methods have not been selected.

## Features should earn their place

For each candidate feature, record the player action, the decision it creates, and the experience it enables. Also identify what must exist for it to work.

| Candidate | A player decision | An experience it could enable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adapting property | Which space to acquire and how to use it. | Discovering a courtyard, opening a home, seeing a familiar block change. |
| People and capabilities | Whom to trust with a responsibility. | Discovering an unexpected talent and watching a familiar person flourish. |
| Gatherings | Venue, invitations, helpers, and scale. | A lively occasion with independent contributions and memorable encounters. |
| Local businesses | Which relationships or projects to support. | A favorite shop becoming part of communal life and traditions. |
| Traditions | What to repeat, name, preserve, or develop. | A community with its own recognizable identity. |
| Visits and travel | Whom to host and which invitations to pursue. | Discovering another place and opening new kinds of relationships. |
| Generational history | Which people, institutions, and relationships to invest in. | Recognizing the effects of earlier choices years later. |

This table is a set of design candidates, not a commitment to implement all of them. Leadership, succession, politics, and worldwide expansion remain part of the broader discussion in the vision notes.

## Suggested order for discussion

Following the mechanics-focused planning correction, use the [game-design principles and tests](game-design-principles.md) as the starting point.

1. Define a candidate core activity with explicit actions, constraints, consequences, and alternative approaches.
2. Make that activity small enough to play repeatedly and evaluate what is enjoyable about doing it.
3. Revise the rules based on what players actually do, discover, and find repetitive.
4. Explore visual and musical treatments alongside this work, with enough presentation to communicate actions and assess the intended feeling.
5. Expand the feature set around mechanics that show promise in play and moments those mechanics can support.

Only after those choices are clearer should this become a production task list. The current work is planning and note-taking.
