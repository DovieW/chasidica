# Vision and player experience

Initial notes: 2026-09-06. Historical exploration: [design v1](../DESIGN.md) now selects the direction. The proposals below preserve the early discussion; the selected specifications govern any differences.

## The promise

Chasidica is a modern Hasidic community and dynasty simulation with a comically ambitious destination: world domination. It begins with a small, recognizable communal world and lets its institutions, relationships, traditions, and ambitions grow to an absurd scale.

The core source of enjoyment should be a rich supply of cool moments and novel experiences: unexpected, joyful, and memorable. A successful campaign should give the player stories they want to tell and places and people they become attached to.

Growth provides new possibilities. Daily life gives that growth meaning.

## What the player sees

The accepted starting direction is a living miniature neighborhood viewed from above. Stylized buildings, pedestrians, deliveries, school dismissal, weddings, and preparations for Yom Tov make it enjoyable to watch.

The player interacts primarily with the mouse. Selecting a building reveals its activities, people, needs, and available actions. Selecting a person reveals their role, relationships, ambitions, and history. Major decisions can appear beside the neighborhood while their physical context remains visible.

Pausable time with play and speed controls is the working proposal. The existing sketch's next-month button demonstrates consequences; it does not decide the final time system.

The city is already inhabited. Properties have owners, shops have customers, other communities have institutions, and residents have their own lives. The player establishes and adapts places within that city.

## Who the player is

The working proposal is to guide an enduring court or dynasty across generations. Individual leaders matter, but the game can continue through death, succession, division, and reconciliation.

The player directs communal resources, projects, appointments, priorities, and relationships. Other people retain agency. Households choose where to live and affiliate; donors choose what to support; teachers and administrators have their own interests. Cooperation, trust, affection, and disagreement all shape what becomes possible.

The exact boundary of the player's authority remains open. We have not decided whether a central playable individual is also needed.

## An opening worth caring about

A promising first ambition is to turn a rented shtiebel into a community with a permanent home.

The neighborhood might initially contain a shul, a small school, a few familiar businesses, and roughly 100 affiliated households. These are scenario suggestions, not balance commitments.

Early physical accomplishments could include:

- Renting and furnishing a storefront.
- Adapting an upstairs apartment into classrooms.
- Buying an adjoining property and connecting the buildings.
- Opening a courtyard that becomes a gathering place.
- Celebrating the opening of a permanent beis medrash.

The first major victory should feel like: we finally own our building.

A small recurring cast could include the rebbe, an energetic administrator, a respected teacher, a donor, a shopkeeper, and a young family. Families and institutions should contain people with distinct perspectives, including women with their own decisions, work, businesses, and relationships.

## An ordinary stretch of play

1. Notice an opportunity or need in the neighborhood: a property becomes available, a visitor arrives, a school fills up, or a resident proposes something.
2. Explore the people and places involved.
3. Choose an ambition and make a plan: find a site, recruit someone, allocate resources, or negotiate help.
4. Let time pass and watch both planned and unexpected developments.
5. Enjoy the result, discover what it made possible, and decide what to pursue next.

The player also acts proactively. Scouting a future neighborhood, supporting a promising teacher, organizing a celebration, and creating a useful shared space should all be worthwhile pursuits.

Quiet observation and celebrations are part of play. A flourishing community needs room to enjoy its successes.

## Long-term growth

The proposed progression moves from one neighborhood to a regional network, international communities, and an increasingly absurd global ambition.

At larger scales, local administrators would handle routine operations while the player directs expansion, relationships, leadership, and exceptional projects. The original neighborhood should remain visitable and recognizable.

Generations can provide continuity: children become adults, founders become elders, institutions develop traditions, and a former pupil may later lead a distant branch. Succession can change the community's future without erasing its past.

World domination is intended as a comic, nonmilitary culmination of institution-building and expansion. Its precise victory condition is open. One candidate is an extravagant shared global project requiring cooperation across a worldwide network.

## Tone and setting

Begin with recognizable communal life and let the scale gradually become ridiculous. Familiar personalities, affectionate disagreements, bureaucracy, and ambitious projects provide comedy.

A transport service might grow from one minibus into an international airline while retaining the name Sunday Minibus Committee. At the opening of its first airport, the original driver still insists he knows a better route.

Warmth, learning, celebration, mutual aid, livelihoods, and belonging deserve attention alongside politics and ambition. Institutions should matter to the people who use them.

A fictionalized New York and fictional courts are the working setting proposal. Specific geography, period, terminology, customs, and differences between communities need further discussion and informed review as content is developed.

## Technical ideas carried forward

Godot 4, a stylized miniature-city aesthetic, reusable building kits, and tunable data-driven simulation were proposed in the initial discussion. Blender could support asset creation later.

These are candidates, not implementation commitments. The current HTML sketch communicates an interaction idea; it does not determine the engine, final graphics, simulation rules, or production architecture.
